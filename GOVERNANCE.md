# SAGE Commons Governance Baseline

This is the pre-foundation governance baseline. It prevents engineering decisions from silently becoming institutional or network authority while the permanent Foundation structure is designed.

- The SAGE Commons Foundation, once constituted, will steward trademarks, releases, the public specification process and shared infrastructure.
- The Foundation does not own users' personal SAGE nodes, private memories or proposer keys and is not a superuser over independent validators.
- Public Commons validator membership, protocol changes and emergency actions require the separately specified network-governance process.
- Maintainers may merge repository changes but cannot use a merge to bypass user consent, validator governance, cryptographic release policy or an accepted RFC.
- Durable cross-subsystem decisions require an RFC; local implementation decisions require an ADR; unresolved product choices remain in the open-decision register.
- Security-critical or protocol changes require at least two qualified reviewers from different operational roles and recorded compatibility/evidence plans.
- Conflicts of interest, maintainer nomination/removal, voting, appeals and Foundation board powers remain open governance decisions and MUST be resolved before public mainnet.

See [`docs/DOCUMENTATION_GOVERNANCE.md`](docs/DOCUMENTATION_GOVERNANCE.md) for the binding engineering-document process.

## Institutional direction under exploration

The owner endorsed exploring a federation of local SAGE organisations on 9 September 2026. The proposed global Foundation supports a shared mission; country, state and community organisations deliver local access and support. Foundation membership, local governance and public-validator operation remain separate roles. Legal forms, representation and voting rules are unresolved.

Federation direction is an owner-endorsed exploration kept in the private `internal-docs/` tree, status IDEA. It grants no new authority and does not amend validator admission, consensus or private-data boundaries. Formal contracts still require the RFC and review process above.
